## AI-Generated Code & Ownership

> **Do I own the code an AI tool generates for me?** Not necessarily. Under current U.S. copyright law, output from purely generative AI lacks a human author and thus **cannot be copyrighted**.

> **Does writing detailed prompts make me the author?** Usually not. Prompts guide the system, but rarely contribute enough creative authorship to secure copyright.

> **Can I copyright AI-generated code?** Only the portions created or significantly modified by a human author qualify. Pure AI output cannot be copyrighted.

> **Can I monetize AI-generated code even if I don't own it?** Yes, but ownership uncertainty increases risk in licensing, fundraising, and exits.

---

## Human Authorship & Mixed Codebases

> **If I edit or refine AI output, do I become the author?** Possibly. Meaningful human revision, selection, or arrangement may establish authorship.

> **How much human input is “enough”?** There is no bright-line rule. Creative contribution and judgment matters more than volume of changes.

> **Can I combine AI-generated and human-written code and own all of it?** You own what *you* create, but not the AI portions unless those portions are replaced or transformed by your human authorship.

> **Does rewriting AI code by hand make it mine?** Not unless the rewrite reflects new creative judgment, not simple transcription.

## Open Source, Licensing & Compliance

> **Can I apply MIT, GPL, or other licenses to AI-generated code?** Applying an open-source license requires you to be the copyright holder. Since pure AI output has no copyright holder, you cannot legally apply an open-source license to that portion.

> **What if AI generates code similar to open-source material?** Similarity alone can still create unwanted licensing obligations (especially for copyleft licenses) or infringement risk, as AI models are trained on existing codebases.

> **Can derivative works based on public code be copyrighted?** Only the new, human-created portions can receive protection.

> **Does AI usage violate open-source terms?** Not inherently, but unmanaged mixing of sources can cause compliance issues.

## Employment, Contractors & Work-for-Hire

> **If an employee uses AI, does the company own the code?** The company owns the human-authored portions via 'work-for-hire,' but the company cannot assert copyright over the purely AI-generated sections.

> **What if a contractor uses AI to deliver code?** Ownership of AI-generated portions may not transfer unless agreements address it explicitly.

> **Should contracts address AI usage?** Yes. Modern agreements should define authorship, ownership, and IP expectations in AI workflows.

---

## Investment, Audits & Exit Strategy

> **Why do investors ask “Who owns the code?”** Ownership clarity directly impacts valuation and defensibility because clear IP is required to legally enforce rights against infringers and secure the company's competitive advantage.

> **Does AI-generated code reduce valuation?** It can. Unowned assets weaken competitive barriers and increase audit risk.

> **What must I disclose during due diligence?** Your AI usage, code provenance, and where human authorship exists.

> **How do I prepare for an audit of AI-generated software?** Maintain lineage, documentation, and evidence of human contributions.

> **What if we don't really care? Customers are paying us for our software, so what if AI created it?** The risk is not in the customer transaction, but in the future viability and value of the company. Lack of ownership means the core code is legally undefensible, exposing the company to competitors, serious licensing and infringement liability, and potentially making the business uninvestable or unsaleable in the future.

---

## Risk Management & Development Best Practices

> **Can engineers still use AI safely?** Yes, when used intentionally and with awareness of ownership implications and documentation best practices.

> **Where is the boundary between AI assistance and authorship?** The boundary is crossed when the developer is merely a scribe. Authorship is established when the human conceptualizes, selects, and arranges the code in a non-trivial, creative manner.

> **What risks arise if AI creates critical code paths?** You may lack enforceable rights, face increased liability concerns, and expose the project to licensing/infringement risk.

> **How should teams document AI usage?** Track prompts, edits, outputs, and decision-making. Clear provenance supports compliance and valuation.
