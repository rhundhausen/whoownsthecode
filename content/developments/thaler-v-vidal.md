In [*Thaler v. Vidal*](https://cafc.uscourts.gov/opinions-orders/21-2347.OPINION.8-5-2022_1988142.pdf), the Federal Circuit held that an "inventor" under the Patent Act must be a natural person, so an AI system cannot be named as the inventor of a patent. The Supreme Court declined to review the decision in 2023.

Patent law reached the same answer as copyright. USPTO guidance permits patents on AI-assisted inventions where a natural person contributed to the conception of the invention; an invention with no human inventive contribution is not patentable. A purely AI-conceived invention is not patentable, and naming a human who did not actually invent it risks invalidating the patent later.
